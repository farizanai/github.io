When does low-rank actually pay? The CapSVD rank rule

November 2026 · low-rank factorization · interactive explainer · ~5 min read

Replacing a weight matrix W with two thin factors A·B is the classic way to compress a layer. But two matrix multiplies are not automatically cheaper than one. This post works through the rule I use in ReSPLIN's CapSVD step to pick a rank that is both accurate enough and actually cheaper.

The break-even rank

A dense projection W ∈ ℝdo×di costs about do·di multiply-accumulates (MACs) per token. A rank-r factorization costs r(di+do). The two are equal at

r*BE = do·di / (di + do)

For a square 2048×2048 attention projection that is just 1024, half the width. Any rank above that is more expensive than leaving the matrix alone. Two kernels instead of one also add launch and memory overhead, so CapSVD keeps a safety margin and caps the rank at γ·r*BE with γ = 0.75.

How much rank does the matrix "need"?

CapSVD first rescales W's input columns by the RMS activation each input dimension sees on calibration data (Weff = W·D), so the SVD focuses on directions the model actually uses. From the singular values of Weff it computes an effective rank, the exponential of the entropy of the normalised spectral energy:

pi = σi² / Σj σj²     reff = exp(−Σi pi log pi)

It then pads that by β = 1.25, rounds up to a multiple of 16 for the hardware, and clamps it with the parameter-reduction target ρc and the break-even cap:

r = min( rmax, max(16, 16·⌈β·reff/16⌉) ),   rmax = min( di, do, rtar, γ·r*BE ),   rtar = (1−ρc)·dodi/(di+do)

Try it

Below is a synthetic singular-value spectrum, σi ∝ i−α. A steeper decay means the energy is concentrated in fewer directions. Move the sliders and watch which constraint decides the final rank.

A few things become obvious once you play with it:

  • For flat spectra the break-even cap binds. When α is small, the effective rank is huge and the padded rank would exceed γ·r*BE. The rule clips it there, which also clips accuracy.
  • For steep spectra the effective rank binds. When the energy is concentrated, a small rank captures most of it, and the MAC saving can be large.
  • Projections that save almost nothing are skipped. CapSVD only keeps candidates with η > 1% parameter reduction, and then factorizes at most Nmax of them, the ones with the largest absolute saving.
What I learned the hard way. In the ReSPLIN ablations, cranking up CapSVD alone reduced parameters steadily, but measured energy per token went flat or up, and throughput didn't improve. At these model sizes the attention projections are already cheap relative to the extra kernel overhead. That is why ReSPLIN uses CapSVD only lightly, on Q and O in the Cap layers, and leaves the energy saving to structured Stem MLP pruning.
