An 80 kB model for mealtime insulin decisions

November 2026 · ML for health · efficient models · IEEE EMBC 2026 · ~5 min read

People with Type 1 diabetes who use multiple daily injections make the same hard decision several times a day: how much insulin should I take for this meal, and how many carbs should I eat, to land in range an hour from now? My EMBC 2026 paper asks whether a very small neural network can give a personalised answer.

Why "personalised" isn't optional

Most ML work in this area forecasts glucose for insulin-pump users. Fewer people work on recommendations for the open-loop, injection-based setting. Rule-based bolus calculators use fixed ratios, but people's glucose behaves very differently. The 12 people in the OhioT1DM dataset I trained on show this clearly:

Two participants average above 180 mg/dL, while others sit comfortably mid-range. Within each person, the spread is ±48–70 mg/dL. A single population-level rule can't fit all of these. So the model is pretrained on everyone and then fine-tuned per person.

What the model is asked to do

Given recent history (CGM glucose, basal and bolus insulin, carbohydrates) and a glucose target at some horizon after the meal, the model recommends the bolus dose and carbohydrate amount. There is also a "without bolus" scenario, for when only carbs can be adjusted. Drag the horizon to see how the task changes:

Small on purpose

The architecture is a convolutional front-end that extracts local temporal features, a GRU that carries state across the window, and the temporal regulariser above. It consistently beat heuristic baselines, Chained-LSTM and N-BEATS on RMSE and MAE, across recommendation scenarios and horizons. It is also much smaller than both neural baselines:

At around 80 kB, the whole model fits comfortably on a microcontroller-class device. A recommendation could be computed on a phone or a CGM receiver, with no cloud round-trip and no health data leaving the device.

Scope. This is a research prototype evaluated retrospectively on a public dataset. It is not a medical device, and any clinical use would need prospective validation and regulatory review.
